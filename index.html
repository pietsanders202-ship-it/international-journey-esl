Stage 01 · Dreaming
Master Open Day · 21 Nov 2026

Maybe Rotterdam?

It starts with a thought. A skyline that looks nothing like the rest of the Netherlands, Europe’s largest port, and a law school where students from 45+ countries study, debate and moot together — in English.

What to expect

Honest from minute one: Erasmus School of Law’s international programmes are master’s level. The LL.M. programmes are fully English-taught; most bachelor programmes are in Dutch. This journey is built for future master students.

On eur.nl
Meet Erasmus School of Law → All master programmes at a glance →
Do I need to speak Dutch? {{ fq.dream_0.icon }}
No. The LL.M. programmes are fully English-taught and the Netherlands is famously fluent in English. Dutch is for bonus points at the market — we’ll teach you ‘lekker’.

02
Stage 02 · Choosing

Find the programme that fits

English-taught LL.M. specialisations — from Maritime & Transport Law (you’re in the right city) to Law & Technology — plus a research master in criminology. Pick by ambition, not by alphabet.

Commercial & Company Law ↗ International Business Law ↗ International Arbitration ↗ International & EU Law ↗ Law & Technology ↗ Maritime & Transport Law ↗ European Master in Law & Economics ↗ IMARC ↗
Reality check · Entry rules
— All LL.M. programmes require a bachelor’s degree in law from a recognised university
—
English proficiency:
TOEFL: A minimum overall score of 90, with at least a score of 20 in each section.
IELTS: A minimum overall score of 6.5, with at least a score of 6.0 in each section.
Cambridge CAE or CPE: A minimum grade of C.
— Only students applying for IEUL (International and European Union Law) or Law & Tech can be considered for admission if they hold a non-law degree. However, they must have completed a substantial number of ECTS credits in law courses.
On eur.nl
All LL.M. programmes & curricula → How to register for your master →
Can I apply to more than one programme? {{ fq.choose_0.icon }}
You can — each application is assessed on its own. Most students end up focusing on the one that genuinely matches their plans.

03
Stage 03 · Applying
Non-EEA? Aim to apply by early spring

Apply & get admitted

Two steps: register in Studielink, the Dutch national portal, then complete your file with EUR — transcripts, English test, motivation. Then the hardest part: waiting.

Reality check · The fine print
— Apply early — after admission, non-EEA students still need months for visa and housing
— Tuition differs: the statutory fee (EU/EEA, roughly €2,700) versus the institutional fee for non-EEA students (roughly €22,000) 
— A Studielink registration alone is not a complete application — finish your document upload
On eur.nl
How to register, step by step → Pick your programme page →
When will I hear back? {{ fq.apply_0.icon }}
Most decisions follow within weeks once your file is complete. Conditional offers (for example pending graduation) are common, so you can keep moving while you finish your bachelor.
Your to-do list
Create your Studielink account
Book your English test now — slots fill up
Collect certified transcripts and diploma
Write a motivation that is specific, not long
Hit submit. Celebrate appropriately.
04
Stage 04 · Preparing
Visa process starts ±12 weeks before day one

Visa, housing & money

Admitted? Fantastic. Now comes the practical sprint — and this is where we hold your hand the tightest, because honesty matters most here.

Reality check · Read this twice
— Housing is genuinely tight. EUR reserves about 1,200 rooms — roughly a quarter of incoming internationals. Register for reserved housing the day it opens.
— Budget around €1,000–1,800 a month for living costs, on top of tuition
— Non-EU? You cannot apply for a residence permit yourself — EUR’s Team Immigration does it for you. Respond fast, every time.
On eur.nl
Housing for international students → Reserved accommodation providers → Visa & residence permit, step by step →
What if I have no room when the semester starts? {{ fq.prepare_0.icon }}
Don’t fly without a room. Widen your search to Schiedam and Capelle aan den IJssel, watch the International Office housing webinars, and email studenthousing@eur.nl — they answer.
Your to-do list
Reply to Team Immigration the day they email
Register for reserved accommodation immediately
Arrange health insurance and proof of finances
Never pay a deposit via Western Union — scams are real
05
Stage 05 · Arriving

Touch down in Rotterdam

Keys in hand, passport stamped. Now the fun bureaucracy: City Hall, bank account, digital campus card — most of it in one go at the One Stop Shop on campus. Then Welcome Day, and a city best learned by bike.

What to expect

The first weeks are a happy blur. You will queue, sign and register — and somewhere in between make half your friends for the year.

On eur.nl
The International Office is your home base → Registering at City Hall →
Will I actually make friends? {{ fq.arrive_0.icon }}
Students from 45+ countries at the law school alone. Between Welcome Day, workgroups and study associations, the real challenge is finding a free evening.
Your to-do list
Register at City Hall (required if you stay 4+ months)
Open a Dutch bank account
Buy a second-hand bike — it’s a personality here
Join the International Welcome Day

06
Stage 06 · Studying

Study like a Rotterdammer

Small-scale, activating education: workgroups where you argue out loud, field trips to courts, law firms and the port. Where law meets business — not as a slogan, but as your Tuesday.

Reality check · Expect to work
— The pace is real: reading-heavy weeks and active participation are the norm, not the exception
— Dutch grading culture: an 8 is excellent and a 10 is folklore — recalibrate early
— Workgroups expect you prepared, in English, out loud
On eur.nl
Life at Erasmus School of Law →
Can I work next to my studies? {{ fq.study_0.icon }}
EU/EEA students can work freely. Non-EU students are limited in hours and the employer needs a permit — plan your budget without counting on a side job.
Your to-do list
Plan your week around the reading list
Join a study association or moot court
Go on the field trips — that’s where it clicks
Struggling? Book your student adviser early, not late
07
Stage 07 · Beyond

Graduation & beyond

Gown on, photo on the bridge. ESL graduates move into international law firms, EU institutions, multinationals, courts and the maritime sector — often via the network they built right here.

What to expect

Want to stay? Non-EU graduates can apply for the orientation year: twelve months to live and work in the Netherlands after graduating, no separate work permit needed.

On eur.nl
Stay connected with ESL → Orientation year — Dutch immigration service (IND) →
What is my degree worth internationally? {{ fq.beyond_0.icon }}
An LL.M. from a research university in the EU, from a school known for where law meets business. Graduates work across Europe and far beyond — the alumni map is half the fun.
